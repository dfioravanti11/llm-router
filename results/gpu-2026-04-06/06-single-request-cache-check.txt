Does a prefix cache hit save measurable time on this hardware, with no router and
no second server involved?

The same prompt, three times, to one idle engine. The first is a miss. The second
and third are hits.

$ P=$(python3 -c "print('the cache is warm when the prefix already lives on this worker. ' * 12)")
$ for i in 1 2 3; do
    curl -s -o /dev/null -w "attempt $i  first byte %{time_starttransfer}s  total %{time_total}s\n" \
      http://localhost:8000/v1/chat/completions \
      -H 'content-type: application/json' \
      -d "{\"model\":\"Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B\",\"max_tokens\":8,\"messages\":[{\"role\":\"user\",\"content\":\"$P\"}]}"
  done

attempt 1  first byte 0.151312s  total 0.151387s
attempt 2  first byte 0.126898s  total 0.126967s
attempt 3  first byte 0.126332s  total 0.126403s

About 190 prompt tokens. A hit saves 25ms, near enough 16% of the request.

The saving is real and it is smaller than the contention two engines add when
they share one device. That is why 02-policy-matrix.txt shows a large hit rate
improvement and no latency improvement.

stopping early:

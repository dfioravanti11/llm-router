Round-robin against prefix-affinity-balanced, on two real vLLM servers.

$ WORKER_URLS="http://127.0.0.1:8000 http://127.0.0.1:8001" \
  MODEL=Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B WORKERS=2 \
  POLICIES="round-robin prefix-affinity-balanced" \
  RATE=5 DURATION=60 WARMUP=10 RUNS=3 CACHE_BLOCKS=112 \
  OUT=results/gpu-matrix ./scripts/policy-compare.sh

Six runs, seeds 101 to 103 per policy, arms interleaved so neither always runs
first. Every run reported valid, with p99 dispatch lag near 2ms.

Per-run time to first token, from intended, in microseconds:

  policy                    seed 101   seed 102   seed 103
  round-robin      mean      33479.8    34976.5    33809.0
  affinity         mean      30514.0    29989.9    29101.9

  round-robin      p50       42815.0    42719.0    42687.0
  affinity         p50       43615.0    43647.0    43327.0

  round-robin      p99       44607.0    44863.0    44575.0
  affinity         p99       45439.0    45279.0    45727.0

=== even workload ===
policy                      throughput   p50 TTFT   p99 TTFT          95% CI on p99  hit rate  busiest
round-robin                     5.2/s     42.7ms     44.6ms        [44.3, 45.1] ms     52.5%      50%
prefix-affinity-balanced        5.2/s     43.6ms     45.4ms        [44.9, 46.0] ms     84.3%      51%

3 run(s) per policy. 'busiest' is the share of requests the busiest
worker took; a third is even across three workers.

Reading it against the mock, which ran three workers on the same workload shape:

  hit rate    round-robin   52.1% mock   52.5% real
  hit rate    affinity      88.4% mock   84.3% real
  p99 TTFT    round-robin   46.0ms mock  44.6ms real
  p99 TTFT    affinity      17.2ms mock  45.4ms real

The cache result reproduced. The tail result did not. The mock predicted affinity
would cut p99 by 2.7x and it did not move at all.

Mean did improve, from 33.8ms to 30.0ms, and affinity won every run with no
overlap between the two groups. That is 11% on a statistic this project does not
otherwise report. It is recorded rather than promoted, because the median and the
tail are what the policy is sold on.

Why the mean moves and the percentiles do not: hit rate is counted in blocks, so
at 84% almost every request is a partial hit rather than a clean one, and every
request still pays to be scheduled. On this machine the cost of being scheduled
is large, because two engines are sharing one device. See
06-single-request-cache-check.txt for the same effect measured without the
contention, where a hit saves 25ms of 151ms.

This run cannot say anything about tail latency under cache-aware routing. One
device cannot show a worker saturating while another idles, and that mechanism is
what the skewed-traffic result depends on.

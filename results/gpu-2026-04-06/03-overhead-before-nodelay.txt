This is not a measurement of router overhead. It is a measurement of a bug.

A cost of 40.17ms with an interval of 0.63ms is not work. Work scales with the
prompt, and a figure that lands on the same tenth of a millisecond three runs
running is a timer. Forty milliseconds is the length of one specific timer: the
Linux delayed acknowledgement, which stalls exactly that long when it meets
Nagle's algorithm on the other side.

Nagle holds a small write back to see whether another is coming. The peer holds
its acknowledgement back for the same reason. Neither moves until the timer
fires. A streaming proxy writes small things constantly, headers and then one
event per token, and the first of those writes is time to first token.

Neither socket had TCP_NODELAY set. Not the connection from the client, not the
connection to the worker.

The direct arm is bimodal, which is the other half of the evidence: p50 of 2.41ms
and p90 of 41.5ms. Roughly 85% of requests bypassing the router return in about
two milliseconds. Every request through the router takes 42ms.

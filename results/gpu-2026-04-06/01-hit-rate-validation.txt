Router policy was prefix-affinity-balanced across both servers. A cache-blind
policy builds no fingerprint and would leave nothing to validate.

$ ROUTER=http://127.0.0.1:8080 \
  WORKERS="http://127.0.0.1:8000 http://127.0.0.1:8001" \
  ./scripts/validate-hit-rate.sh

prefix cache hit rate
  router predicted    85.06%   over 5,634 blocks routed
  workers reported    76.15%   over 92,959 blocks looked up
  gap                 +8.91%

VERDICT: the router is optimistic. It believed the workers held more
than they did, so requests were routed for hits that did not happen.

Two notes on reading this.

The script labelled both totals "blocks". Only one of them is. vLLM counts
tokens. 5,634 blocks at 16 tokens each is 90,144 tokens against the engines'
92,959, a 3% difference, which means both sides saw the same traffic. The 16x
disagreement it appears to show is a unit error in the label, not in the data.
Both rates are fractions, so the comparison of rates was always sound. The script
was corrected afterwards to name the units and to warn when the two totals drift
far enough apart to mean the sides saw different requests.

The +8.9 point gap is real. The likely causes are the in-flight reservation,
which credits a worker with blocks a dispatched request has not finished writing,
and an eviction model that is the router's own rather than vLLM's.

What this settles: the router's prompt rendering, tokenizer, and block hash chain
agree with a real engine. A worker cannot report 76% on this traffic otherwise.
